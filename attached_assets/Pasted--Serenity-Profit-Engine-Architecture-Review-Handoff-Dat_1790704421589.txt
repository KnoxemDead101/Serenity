Its purpose is becoming:

> Bring the user's finances, financial planning, investments, portfolio management, trading preparation, trade execution records, journaling, and financial decision-making into one structured workspace.

5. What assets and investments do I own?

11. Did I follow my plan?
12. What was my actual risk and reward?
13. Is my trading strategy working over time?
14. Where is my financial plan currently heading?
15. How would changes to my financial assumptions affect that future?

Serenity should organize these systems without incorrectly treating all financial activity as the same thing.

# 5. Core Architecture Rules — Preserve These

The following are non-negotiable unless a concrete technical defect is identified and reviewed first.

## Financial arithmetic

Never use ordinary floating-point storage for financial values.

Preserve accurate integer/fixed-precision representations.

## Store facts, calculate results

Do not store derived values merely because they are convenient to display.

Examples:

Account balance should continue to be calculated from its underlying facts.

Future open trading quantity should be derived from fills.

Portfolio cost basis should be derived from investment activity where practical.

## Actual ≠ Projected ≠ Scenario

Actual financial records must remain separate from projections and hypothetical scenarios.

Scenarios must never overwrite actual records.

## Investing ≠ Trading

Long-term investing and active trading are different financial domains.

They may share instruments and interact through real capital movement, but they should not be modeled as identical activities.

## Trading profit remains in trading capital

Trading P&L does not automatically become checking-account income.

Money changes financial location only when an actual withdrawal/transfer occurs.

## Prop-firm buying power is not net worth

Prop account buying power is not a personal financial asset.

Fees paid and payouts actually received can affect real finances.

## Workspace ownership

Every new financial entity must belong to the appropriate Serenity workspace either directly or through a relationship whose authorization cannot be bypassed.

## Thin routes

Keep financial/business logic in services.

Do not turn API routes into large business-logic modules.

## No financial calculations in JavaScript

Frontend code should collect/display information, not become the authoritative financial calculation engine.

# 16. Market Levels and Zones Persist Across Trading Days

This requirement is important.

Markets are continuous.

A supply zone, demand zone, support/resistance level, or area of interest may remain relevant across several trading sessions.

Do not make levels children that cease to exist when a Trading Day ends.

Example:

```text
NQ Demand Zone

Created: Monday
Reviewed: Tuesday
Active: Wednesday
Tested: Thursday
Invalidated: Friday
```

Initial lifecycle should remain intentionally simple:

```text
ACTIVE
TESTED
INVALIDATED
ARCHIVED
```

Evaluate whether Level and Zone should be separate models or one generalized market-area representation.

Do not over-engineer classification in v1.

# 20. Trade Execution Must Support Real Scaling

Do not design the Trade model around only:

```text

```

Design for:

```text
Single Entry → Single Exit

Single Entry → Multiple Exits

Multiple Entries → Single Exit

Multiple Entries → Multiple Exits
```

Scaling in and scaling out must be supported.

Conceptually:

```text
Trade
├── Instrument
├── Direction
├── Plan relationship
├── Risk context
├── Status
└── Trade Fills
    ├── Entry Fill
    ├── Entry Fill
    ├── Exit Fill
    └── Exit Fill
```

Serenity should derive weighted-average entry and exit prices.

Do not require the user to calculate them manually.

# 23. Trading Math Service

This is one of the most important components of the proposed architecture.

Trading calculations must not assume:

```text
1 point = $1
```

That is false for many futures instruments.

Instrument specifications must drive conversion.

For futures, the system needs concepts such as:

- tick size;
- tick value;
- point value;
- contract quantity;
- instrument specification/version.

The authoritative Trading Math Service should derive appropriate values from underlying facts.

Potential derived calculations include:

- price difference;
- points;
- ticks;
- dollar value per contract;
- gross P&L;
- fees;
- net P&L;
- point risk;
- tick risk;
- dollar risk;
- point reward;
- dollar reward;
- planned risk/reward;
- realized R.

Support both LONG and SHORT calculations.

Do not perform authoritative trading math in JavaScript.

---

# 24. Mini vs Micro Futures

Mini and micro contracts cannot share hardcoded point-dollar assumptions.

The instrument specification must determine conversion.

The same number of market points can represent very different dollar risk depending on instrument and contract quantity.

Tests must explicitly prove this.

# 25. Planned vs Actual Risk

Preserve planned information separately from actual execution.

Conceptually:

```text
PLANNED

planned entry
planned stop
planned target
planned quantity
planned point risk
planned dollar risk
planned R:R
```

versus:

```text
ACTUAL

fills
actual average entry
actual average exit
actual quantity
gross P&L
fees
net P&L
realized R
```

This allows Serenity to evaluate execution quality rather than only profit/loss.

# 26. Historical Instrument Specifications

Do not silently make this decision during implementation.

# 41. Product Philosophy

Serenity should eventually answer a much larger question than:

> What did I spend?

> What do I own?

> What am I planning?

> What requires my attention?

> What financial decisions am I making?

> Why did I make them?

> What actually happened?

> Did I follow my system?

The purpose is not to automate financial judgment.

# 42. FINAL POINT FOR CLAUDE — DEVELOPMENT DISCIPLINE

This application is also being used as a software-engineering learning project.

Do not let AI-generated complexity outrun the developer's ability to understand and maintain Serenity.

For every proposed major component, explain:

1. **What problem it solves.**
2. **Why a new component/model is necessary.**
3. **What inputs it receives.**
4. **What it stores versus what it calculates.**
5. **What output it produces.**
6. **Which files/layers should own the behavior.**
7. **How the developer can manually test it.**
8. **What software-engineering concept the implementation demonstrates.**

Prefer understandable architecture over impressive architecture.

Do not introduce:

- unnecessary abstraction;
- unnecessary infrastructure;
- unnecessary patterns;
- generic frameworks for hypothetical future requirements;
- large opaque modules;
- dependencies simply because they are convenient for AI.

New infrastructure must solve a demonstrated Serenity requirement and remain explainable by the developer.

A good Serenity implementation is not one where AI generated the most code.

A good implementation is one where:

```text
The developer can trace it.
The developer can explain it.
The developer can test it.
The developer can troubleshoot it.
The developer can modify it.
The developer can deploy it.
The developer can recover it.
```

Use one tested vertical slice at a time.

Preserve educational comments and readability.

When recommending a pattern the existing project does not currently use, explain why the existing architecture is insufficient before introducing the new pattern.
